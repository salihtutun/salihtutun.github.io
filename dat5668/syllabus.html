The course meets in person in Simon Hall, Room 00122, Mondays and Wednesdays at 4:00 pm, for about 80 minutes. There are seven modules and 13 meetings: Modules 1–6 each have a concept session and a practice session. Module 7 is the project session. Passing time between labs is 10 minutes. Prerequisites

Graduate: DAT 5565 (Deep Learning for Predictions of Business Outcomes) or DAT 6565, or instructor permission. Proposed BSBA cross-listing: DAT 5565 or equivalent preparation (Python plus supervised machine learning and deep learning), or instructor permission. There are no co-requisites. MSA and BSBA students follow the same sessions and the same labs.

The course is applied AI for business. It is proposed for the BSBA Major in AI and the Minor in Business Analytics and AI. Students leave able to design, evaluate, and govern an agent: tool use, grounding, Markov decision processes, Q-learning, deep Q-networks, hybrids, and a managerial scorecard. They also leave with a reusable agent artifact for a business process they choose. That is the work hiring teams ask for. Students who want large-scale systems or compiler-level work should take a McKelvey elective.

Why both families are in one course

The agent receives a state, sends an action to an environment, and gets a reward or penalty plus the next state. That is reinforcement learning, and it is the loop of this course. A language model can sit in the same decide step and call tools. Students already know Python and supervised models from DAT 5565; those models help form the state or are tools, they are not the agent. Evaluation against a baseline is required. The course teaches LLM-based and RL-based agents because a firm has to choose: language and documents, a repeated numeric decision, a hybrid, or no automation. Odd sessions teach the idea; even sessions practice it in the room. For the project, the group picks any real business problem, designs a reusable agent artifact, and runs the loop on simulated data, public data, or a free API from public-apis. The project may be an LLM agent, an RL agent, or a hybrid, and it must state the loop, justify the family, compare a baseline, and include a short governance note.

Materials and software

Bring a laptop. Labs use Google Colab, Python, NumPy, and Matplotlib. The notebooks run with a local tool-calling stand-in, so class does not require an API key. If an instructor enables Gemini for a session, the course key is used in class and the local stand-in remains. For the project, you may call a free API listed in public-apis; prefer rows marked No under Auth. You may also call a fitted sklearn, XGBoost, or DAT 5565 Keras model as a tool; a prediction-only notebook is not the project. Do not paste confidential data into a public tool.

For the reinforcement-learning sessions, the main book is Richard S. Sutton and Andrew G. Barto, Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction, 2nd ed., MIT Press, 2018 (print, or the free author PDF and HTML). Optional: Enes Bilgin, Mastering Reinforcement Learning with Python, Packt, 2020. LLM-agent sessions use the instructor notebooks and selected readings on tool use and retrieval. You are assessed on both families. The free Sutton and Barto text is the book of record. Do not rely on a copied PDF from a third party.

Description

Agentic AI is the design of systems that act toward a goal in an environment: they take a state, send an action, and receive a reward or penalty and the next state. LLM agents fit knowledge work: retrieval, tools, multi-step workflows, and handoffs, still inside that loop. RL agents fit repeated sequential control, such as inventory or pricing. Industry systems often combine them: a language model plans or handles exceptions, a learned policy executes a repeated decision, and a fitted machine-learning model may score the state. A chatbot is a poor inventory controller. A deep Q-network is a poor contract reviewer. A prediction-only DAT 5565 model is not an agent until it sits in this loop.

Learning objectives

  • Explain the shared loop as state, action, environment, reward, and next state, and place an LLM or a DAT 5565 model inside that loop as a policy or a tool.
  • Build a prototype LLM agent with tool use and retrieval, and evaluate grounding, cost, and human oversight.
  • Formulate a sequential business problem as a Markov decision process and apply tabular Q-learning, including ε-greedy exploration.
  • Build and train a deep Q-network with experience replay, diagnose failure modes, and compare it with a baseline.
  • Evaluate both families on one scorecard against a baseline, then combine and govern them in a short note for managers.
  • Leave with a reusable agent artifact for a business process they choose, ready to run on a new day of work.

How to do well

  • Attend each session and take part. If the work is not landing, come to office hours.
  • Practice the notebook examples until you can name the problem type and write the result so a manager can use it.
  • Plan on about six hours a week outside class, plus time to get comfortable in Python. That varies by person.
  • Ask in the session when something is unclear.

Olin students embody a values-based and data-driven ethos, understand global opportunities and challenges, apply experiential knowledge with technical skill, and pursue work with an entrepreneurial mindset.

Attendance and conduct

Arrive on time and be ready to work. You are responsible for material covered if you miss a session. Email the instructor if you know you will be absent, or as soon as you can after an unexpected absence. Notes for the course are the session notebooks and this site.

A positive room depends on arriving on time, listening when someone else is speaking, and using professional language. Phones and unrelated browsing stay away during class. A laptop or tablet for the notebook and for notes is appropriate. Recording the class requires the instructor’s prior approval. No recording device is otherwise permitted.

Except as authorized by the instructor or the university, students may not record, stream, reproduce, display, publish, or further distribute classroom activities or course materials. This includes lectures, discussions, office hours, assessments, answers, slides, and screenshots. A student who needs assistive technology as an accommodation contacts the Office of Disability Resources first. If recording is permitted, unauthorized use or distribution of that recording is still prohibited.

Academic integrity

The Olin Business School expects integrity in academic work. In all academic work, the ideas and contributions of others, including generative artificial intelligence, must be acknowledged, and work presented as original must be original. Read the academic integrity policy for your program and the Olin Code of Conduct. Ask before you are unsure: what collaboration means, and what a quiz allows.

  • Quizzes are your own work.
  • Discussion of homework and of ungraded class material is encouraged. The file you submit is your own.
  • Signing attendance for someone else, or asking someone to sign for you, is prohibited.
  • Passing notes, handouts, or solutions to students who have not yet taken the course, or to another section, is prohibited. An archive of past materials beyond what the instructor provides is prohibited.
  • Attending a different section without prior approval is prohibited.
  • Every member of a group contributes. Every member is responsible for the integrity of the group work.
  • Plagiarism is a violation. Cite sources.
  • Submitting similar work in more than one class requires approval from every instructor involved.

Generative AI tools such as Copilot, ChatGPT, or DeepSeek are not permitted unless an assignment says so. Using the course notebooks, including the local tool loop or a course-approved model when the lab requires it, is part of the assignment. Submitting an AI-written report as your own, using unauthorized AI on a quiz, or pasting confidential data into a public tool is not. You may tell the instructor, anonymously if you wish, if you suspect improper behavior. An indicated honor-code violation goes to the disciplinary process.

Papers and assignments may be submitted through Turnitin. If you do not use Turnitin, the instructor may upload the work for review.
