Part 1: What is the coin a claim on? (1 page)

Write so a careful reader could understand the object.

  1. Is the token the thing itself, or a receipt (coat-check stub) for something off-chain?
  2. Redemption: who honors it, in what (CLASSUSD, a cookie, minutes, nothing), within what time.
  3. If the team disappears: what do holders actually have? Be honest (often: a PDF and a grade problem, not a court claim).
  4. Atomic vs fail: one sentence on how a pool swap (both sides or neither) differs from “I sent TEAM, you forgot the notes.”

In Session 9 you must honor one redemption in the room. Record the tx hash and/or a photo of the handoff. Two proofs beat one slogan: explorer hash + honor evidence.

Part 2: Obligation matrix (1 page + table)

Use real Module 4 trades if you have them. If not, label the triangle instructor example.

  1. Matrix: rows = who owes, columns = who is owed, cells = CLASSUSD-equivalent.
  2. Find one cycle and erase it (if Ava, Ben, and Cara each owe 10 around the triangle, you need zero payments for those 10s).
  3. Write what is left after the erase. (If Ava owed 15 instead of 10, leftover is Ava → Ben = 5.)
  4. Name how the leftover settles (CLASSUSD rail, TEAM, or another class rail you can point to).
  5. One paragraph: would a public digital dollar (CBDC-like) help this class or crowd out team coins? Pick a side. CLASSUSD is still not a CBDC.

Netting saves rails. It does not make uneven promises disappear.
