SAMPLE Final Project memo (laundry : not Eagecoin)

This is a SAMPLE of a full Final Project memo. It is not EAGE and not your team’s coin. Use it to see the shape: business audience, eight headings, numbers, unbundle, go/partner/pass with a kill number and a compliance line. Write your own facts from A1-A6.

Word count (body): about 1,800-2,000 target shape. Appendix hashes do not count.

To: Head of Product; CAO / campus-ops partner track
From: Sample Team (laundry floor pilot)
Re: SPIN rail : go / partner / pass
Date: Demo day sample

1. The problem and the community

The community is one WashU dorm floor (about 40 residents) that shares six washers and four dryers. The problem is not “we want a blockchain.” The problem is double-booking and unpaid make-goods: two people claim the same Thursday 8 p.m. dryer, and the person who left a wet load overnight never pays the $2 detergent fee they promised in the group chat. Chat screenshots are not a ledger. The RA clipboard is a ledger, but it does not travel with the resident and it does not show a stranger that a fee moved.

A licensed campus-ops firm already sells work-order software to housing. They care because every missed slot becomes a ticket, a refund argument, and a Resident Advisor (RA) hour. Housing directors measure those hours. Controllers care when detergent fees sit in a cash box with no tie to who owed them. Assurance cares when a “digital laundry coin” shows up in a pilot budget with no clear claim definition.

If a rail can show who held a slot and whether the fee moved, the firm can cut reconciliation between the chat and the RA clipboard. If it cannot : or if the only “innovation” is wrapping a spreadsheet in a token : the firm should ignore the token and keep the spreadsheet. That is the business question this memo answers.

2. The mechanism

Who mints. Only the RA multisig (two of three RAs) may mint SPIN. Cap is 10,000 SPIN. Premint at deploy: 2,000 to the floor pool, 2,000 to a detergent treasury, 200 × 30 active residents = 6,000 airdropped once (A3 runbook). Pause role: same RA multisig. Instructor is not on the mint key. A controller map would list: issuer = RA ops role; pauser = same; no public minter; no automatic inflation.

What one SPIN means. One SPIN is a claim on one 30-minute dryer slot on that floor’s schedule, or a $2 CLASSUSD detergent fee at the posted redemption desk (A5). It is not a share of the dorm, not a profit right, and not a public sale. On the books, treat it as a prepaid service credit for that floor : not as an investment asset.

Claim / redemption. Resident shows wallet balance + QR from the booking sheet. RA burns 1 SPIN (or records redemption in the log) and either (a) unlocks the dryer calendar row or (b) hands a detergent packet and marks CLASSUSD out of treasury. Failed delivery (broken dryer) → RA refunds SPIN within 24 hours or the kill rule fires (heading 7). Delivery-versus-payment here is simple: burn happens when the calendar stamp or detergent handoff happens, not when someone “likes” a chat message.

An assurer can vouch balances and burns on the explorer. They cannot vouch that the dryer actually heated without the RA photo or the calendar stamp. That split is the reporting point: on-chain evidence supports existence and transfer of the credit; off-chain evidence still supports performance of the laundry service. A Monday-morning assurance memo would say exactly that : and would refuse to treat a Sepolia balance alone as proof that housing delivered a dry load.

4. What the market revealed

The class bowl (A4 + L2) started at 2,000 SPIN / 1,000 CLASSUSD. After three peer swaps and one detergent redemption, spot moved to about 0.47-0.52 CLASSUSD per SPIN. Depth stayed thin: one 100 SPIN buy moved the quote by more than 8%. Impermanent loss for the RA pool was visible but small in CLASSUSD terms because volume was class-scale, not market-scale. Average price across the lab window was near 0.50; spot swung wider than average, which is the usual thin-book pattern.

Risk language. Price is a dance partner for CLASSUSD in this room, not a fair value for a housing P&L. Who traded: classmates and one RA testing redemption : not external market makers. A product lead should read this as proof of mechanism, not proof of a liquid market. Thin book is expected; it is not a listing pitch. If a deck said “SPIN trades at a stable 50 cents,” that would be hype. The honest line is: the bowl cleared a few classroom swaps and showed how fast a shallow pool moves.

5. Unbundle the stack

We needed a shared list of who had a Thursday laundry slot. A Google Sheet with an RA as registrar would have done that job: one person edits, everyone reads. We did not need a global consensus computer to stop two people booking the same dryer : the RA already sits in the building.

We rented Sepolia anyway, so we borrowed someone else’s consensus. That was a rail, not the product. The product was the booking rule and the detergent fee.

We did want a token rather than a verbal “I’ll save you a machine,” because classmates outside the floor had to pay and we had to show a tx on demo day. An escrow tab (“RA holds a sticky note”) would have been enough inside the dorm. The token earned its keep only when a stranger swapped SPIN for CLASSUSD in the class bowl : that is a market object, not a chat IOU.

The unbundle is the MIT outcome of this course in miniature: name which layer did the financial work. For laundry, the registrar and the claim definition did the work. Consensus was rented. A firm that cannot say that sentence will overbuy chain and underbuy process.

6. What broke

On Week 4 lab night, one resident’s MetaMask was on the wrong network. She showed a Sepolia balance of zero while the registry said she held 200 SPIN. The RA almost re-airdropped. The ops story: always match network + registry address before minting again. We fixed it by switching networks and refreshing the explorer : no second mint. A risk memo would keep that as a control failure (wrong chain view), not as “blockchain is hard.”

A second small break: a dryer went offline after Ben burned SPIN for a slot. The 24-hour make-good rule forced a refund burn reverse in the log and a calendar note. That is the kind of ops story a firm should expect before they copy any rail : tokens do not fix broken machines; kill rules and human process do.

7. Go / partner / pass

Partner.

A licensed campus-ops shop should partner on the booking list and redemption desk (they already have work-order databases and RA workflows). They should pass on deploying a public-chain token for laundry slots. Copy the kill rule; do not copy the chain as the product. Go would be wrong: there is no licensed need to put dryer slots on a public consensus computer. Pass on the whole idea would also be wrong: the booking-list and fee-desk problems are real, and the firm’s existing software can absorb them without a token.

Kill number (from A6/L3 shape). If a reserved slot is not honored within 24 hours, pause mint and stop new bookings until the RA posts a make-good. If \|spot − 0.50\| / 0.50 > 40% and pool depth < 50 CLASSUSD, pause the bowl (classroom market only : not a housing valuation trigger).

Compliance line. No public sale of SPIN; no profit pitch; RA mint is an ops role, not an investment manager. Under a Howey-shaped classroom score, we cut any “list SPIN and 10×” language (A6 Part 2 fire drill). Travel Rule does not apply to this dorm fee rail; if the firm ever moved real value off-campus, they would need their own transfer-rule program : that is out of scope for laundry.

8. Sources

  1. Sweeney and Sweeney (1977) / Krugman babysitting co-op : shortage of scrip freezes real activity; our SPIN airdrop is scrip for slots, not a growth narrative. When scrip is scarce, real coordination freezes; when scrip is confused with investment, the wrong controls get built.
  2. Naranjo : AMM / thin-book intuition: small trades move the quote when depth is low (matches our L2 bowl). Classroom volume is not market evidence.
  3. MIT DCI / BIS framing : separate the ledger rail from the business claim; do not treat consensus as the product when a registrar already exists. That separation is the Monday-morning skill this course trains.

Business use of this skill. In public accounting you learn what to vouch on-chain vs off-chain (heading 3). In controllership you map mint/claim roles (heading 2). In consulting you say partner/pass with a kill number (heading 7). On a real Monday, that looks like: an assurance team refusing to treat a wallet screenshot as a complete inventory count; a controller coding a prepaid laundry credit correctly; a product advisor telling housing to buy workflow software and ignore a public-chain laundry token. That is why ACCT 5105 exists : not so you ship another coin.
