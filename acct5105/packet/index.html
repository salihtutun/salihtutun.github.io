1. Course overview

ACCT 5105 is financial reporting and assurance in a blockchain. You leave able to read a shared ledger the way you read a set of books: what was recorded, what it is worth, and what an assurer can stand behind.

The course sits at the intersection of financial reporting, assurance, and blockchain. Distributed ledgers change how finance keeps the books. You will learn the architecture that matters for that work: decentralization, cryptographic security, immutability, and transparency, and how those features change the preparation, verification, and audit of financial statements. You will also see what blockchain does to internal controls, audit procedures, and regulatory compliance.

The hands-on case is a class testnet coin, Eagecoin (ticker EAGE), priced against instructor-issued CLASSUSD. You record it, trace it, and assure it the way you would a digital-asset balance — not as a product pitch. Testnet only. No mainnet and no real money.

2. Learning objectives

By the end of the course you can:

  1. Blockchain fundamentals. Explain a distributed ledger, a consensus mechanism, and a block structure, including how a transaction is recorded and linked so it cannot be quietly rewritten.
  2. Financial reporting. Say where blockchain can improve accuracy, transparency, and real-time validation of financial data, and where it cuts reconciliation — and where it does not.
  3. Audit and assurance. Use the chain for verification, fraud signals, and risk assessment, and say which traditional audit procedures have to change.
  4. Regulation and ethics. Apply accounting standards, legal frameworks, and the auditor’s ethical duties when the records are on a chain.
  5. Practice. Complete case work and notebook exercises that connect a blockchain record to an accounting information system: issue, circulate, measure, and then report what an assurer would rely on.

How we make the ideas stick. Every session notebook opens with Words we use today, runs Demo cells you execute, then YOUR TURN, and closes with Check you understand. The longer explanation lives in the concept handbook. Use it if you missed class or a word still will not land (about 10 minutes).

3. Who should take this

Program. ACCT 5105-01 is a course in Washington University’s Olin Business School Master of Science in Business Analytics, with a focus on financial reporting and assurance in a blockchain world. You bring accounting judgment together with SQL, Python, R, and data analytics.

Who it is for. You will advise on digital-asset accounting and blockchain-based transactions, read what a shared ledger does to reporting and compliance, and say what an assurance procedure can and cannot verify.

Where it leads. Public accounting, corporate finance, and consulting in a digital-first environment.

Prerequisites. Comfort with financial statements and with a Python notebook. SQL or R is useful and not assumed in every lab. No prior Solidity. No prior crypto required.

Out of scope. Writing production smart contracts from scratch, mining hardware, token-price forecasting, mainnet, or treating every database problem as a blockchain problem.

10. Integrity and conduct

  • Labs: discussion allowed; written answers must be your own.
  • Quizzes: individual, closed notes.
  • A7: teammates may share logs and screenshots; one memo for the team. A member who cannot explain the work at the demo can receive a different score.
  • A seed phrase in a submission is a security fail (points off and a private reset), not a clever appendix.
  • Mainnet deploy or a public sale for this course is a zero on the project.
